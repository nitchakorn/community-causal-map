What Should Change in Bowling Green? — Sensemaker Recreation (200-comment sample)

Executive Summary

  • 110 Total Participants
  • 8 Topics Identified
  • 54 Opinions Identified

This report summarizes the results of public input from 110 participants. From the participant responses, 8 high level topics were identified, as well as 54 opinions. All participants were anonymous.

Residents envision a self-sustaining Bowling Green that balances rapid growth with neighborhood preservation. To manage this expansion, advocates urge leaders to transition from car-centric infrastructure to walkable, green networks while retaining local wealth and university graduates through targeted economic incentives.

This desire for local self-determination shapes how residents define public accountability and safety. While some groups champion corporate expansion to capture regional revenue, others prioritize small businesses and demand direct oversight of zoning and taxation. Similarly, participants seek consistent public safety and code enforcement but insist on reactive, complaint-driven systems to protect personal privacy and prevent government overreach.

Conversation Overview

Below is a high level overview of the topics discussed in the conversation. The most discussed topics were Urban Planning and Neighborhood Development and Infrastructure and Transportation Management.

Urban Planning and Neighborhood Development

5 Opinions
29 Total quotes

Residents want a vibrant, walkable city that balances rapid growth with neighborhood preservation. A major priority is transforming the downtown and riverfront into active, green public spaces. To protect older neighborhoods, residents favor home improvement grants, stricter property maintenance, and zoning that limits high-density housing in single-family areas. Integrating green spaces and improving walkability are key. While most focus on improving city-led planning, some propose a shift in power: requiring formal consent from a majority of nearby neighbors before any zoning changes can occur.

Infrastructure and Transportation Management

3 Opinions
28 Total quotes

Bowling Green's rapid growth is outpacing its infrastructure. Residents urge a shift from car-centric roads to a safer network for drivers, cyclists, and walkers. Traffic bottlenecks dominate concerns. Drivers want smart signals, widened roads, and solutions for blocked rail crossings. Downtown, scarce parking hurts businesses, while rural areas lack water lines and high-speed internet. Expanding bus routes, linking fragmented bike paths, and building sidewalks would protect pedestrians. Leaders must balance these immediate road fixes with long-term utility and transit expansion.

20 Total quotes

Residents want a healthier, greener community. The loudest call is for more play and social spaces for kids, teens, and seniors. Connecting fragmented trails and developing the Barren River waterfront would link neighborhoods to nature. Residents also push for city-wide recycling, litter cleanup, and green parking lots. However, a key tension exists: should the city build structured sports complexes or preserve quiet, natural parks?

Public Safety and Legal Enforcement

8 Opinions
17 Total quotes

Residents want local authorities to focus on consistent, fair enforcement that protects daily quality of life and safety. The strongest demands center on cracking down on dangerous driving and neighborhood nuisances like noise and loose animals. However, residents draw a line at code enforcement, preferring a reactive, complaint-based system to avoid harassment. Beyond daily policing, the community seeks systemic fairness. This includes equal legal treatment regardless of social status, rehabilitation programs for non-violent inmates, and more diverse police hiring to build trust.

Education and Youth Development

8 Opinions
15 Total quotes

Residents see education as the key to keeping Bowling Green thriving and healthy. The main goal is keeping local talent. Stronger ties with Western Kentucky University would help keep graduates nearby through internships and teaching jobs. To help all families, residents want more learning choices. These include public Montessori classrooms, transition programs for immigrant youth, and financial aid for private schools. Learning must also extend beyond the classroom. Residents want better youth spaces, like a southern Warren County library. They also call for detailed sex education and expanded arts funding.

Community Health and Social Services

14 Total quotes

Residents link community health directly to physical access and hands-on support. Key needs include opening grocery stores in neighborhoods like Delafield and building a local trauma center to avoid long-distance emergency transfers. Economic Growth and Workforce Opportunities

7 Opinions
13 Total quotes

Bowling Green can boost its economy by keeping talent and wealth local. Residents want to fill 6,500 open jobs through targeted training, like inmate manufacturing programs, and retain WKU graduates with startup grants. Fixing run-down areas like the bypass and Louisville Road will capture regional cash. Public fiber internet could power this growth. However, strategies clash. Some push to attract big brands like Costco to stop shopping trips to Nashville. Others demand the city prioritize local small businesses for tax incentives and tie these perks to living wages.

Governance and Civic Engagement

7 Opinions
10 Total quotes

Residents seek a direct link between their financial contributions and their influence over local decisions. To dismantle informal power networks, the community urges leaders to prioritize transparency and cautious, demand-driven growth. This push for accountability spans several areas. Annexed residents expect services that match their taxes, and neighbors want veto power over zoning changes. Friction persists around representation, from unelected school boards raising taxes to county workers lacking a vote in city politics. Residents also favor reactive, complaint-driven code enforcement over proactive policing.
